CHAPTER I. Karakter dan Kegiatan Ksatria Terkenal Don Quixote de la Mancha

Di sebuah desa di La Mancha, whose name I do not care to call to mind, there lived not long since one of those gentlemen that keep a lance in the lance-rack, an old buckler, a lean hack, and a greyhound for coursing.

An olla of rather more beef than mutton, a salad on most nights, scraps on Saturdays, lentils on Fridays, and a pigeon or so extra on Sundays, made away with three-quarters of his income.

He spent the remainder of it in a doublet of fine cloth and velvet breeches and shoes to match, for holidays, while on week-days he made a brilliant figure in his best homespun.

In short, his household consisted of a housekeeper past forty, a niece under twenty, and a lad for the field and market-place, who used to saddle the horse as well as handle the hoe.

CHAPTER II. Sally Pertama dari Sang Ksatria Jenius Don Quixote

Having thus made his preparations, he was anxious to lose no time in putting them into execution, and the opportunity soon presented itself. One morning, before it was yet light, he rose, seized his lance, and went out into the yard to exercise himself a little with his horse Rosinante.

He had not been riding long when he came to a plain where he espied a number of windmills, which, as is the custom in La Mancha, are placed at short intervals all over the country.

"Look there, friend Sancho," said Don Quixote, as soon as he perceived them; "there are thirty or forty monstrous giants, with whom I mean to fight, and take all their lives at once."

CHAPTER III. Di Mana Dijelaskan Cara Dahi Don Quixote Menjadi Ksatria

The innkeeper, who saw Don Quixote lying on the ground, thought he was dead, and ran to fetch a candle to see what had happened. When he came back with the light, he found Don Quixote sitting up, rubbing his head with a handkerchief, and looking very foolish.

"What has happened to you, sir?" asked the innkeeper. "Are you hurt?"

"Hurt!" exclaimed Don Quixote. "I am not hurt in the least. That villain Freston, the enchanter, has carried off my horse, and left me here on foot."

The innkeeper could not forbear laughing at the sight of Don Quixote's wounded head and serious countenance. He told him that there were no giants nor enchanters in the inn, but only a company of merchants who had come to attend the fair.

CHAPTER IV. Apa yang Terjadi pada Ksatria Kami Ketika Dia Meninggalkan Penginapan

Don Quixote, having concluded his adventure at the inn, pursued his journey, and soon found himself in the open country, where he encountered a company of Yanguesans, who were driving a flock of sheep to market.

The moment he saw them, he exclaimed, "There is a host of infidels coming to attack me! Sancho, my friend, draw your sword, and help me to defend myself."

Sancho Panza, who was very unwilling to fight, tried to dissuade his master, but all in vain. Don Quixote charged full tilt among the sheep, and began to lay about him with his lance.

CHAPTER V. Nasib Ksatria Kami Dilanjutkan

The shepherds, seeing their flock being destroyed by a madman, fell upon Don Quixote with their slings and stones, and would soon have put an end to his adventures, had it not been for the arrival of a neighbor, who recognized him and came to his rescue.

When Don Quixote recovered his senses, he found himself lying on the ground, sorely bruised and wounded. "Sancho," said he, "I think I have been enchanted again, for I feel as if every bone in my body were broken."

"Indeed, sir," replied Sancho, "I think you have been more than enchanted, for the shepherds have beaten you most soundly."

CHAPTER VI. Pemeriksaan Menarik yang Dilakukan oleh Kurus dan Tukang Cukur di Perpustakaan Ksatria Kami

The curate and the barber, hearing of Don Quixote's illness, came to visit him, and found him in bed, very weak and melancholy. They examined his library, which was full of books of chivalry, and concluded that this was the cause of his madness.

"This must be remedied," said the curate. "These books of knight-errantry have turned his brain, and we must burn them all."

They selected a number of books to be burned, and the rest were locked up, so that Don Quixote might not have access to them.

CHAPTER VII. Sally Kedua dari Ksatria Berjasa Kami

Don Quixote, having recovered from his illness, determined to make a second sally. He chose a new squire, Sancho Panza, and set out one evening, without taking leave of anyone, to seek adventures in the fields of La Mancha.

They had not gone far when they heard a great noise, which Don Quixote imagined to be the groans of some distressed damsel. He charged towards the sound, and found a flock of sheep being attacked by a wolf.

"Hold, monster!" cried Don Quixote, and threw his lance at the wolf. But the sheep, frightened by the noise, began to run in all directions.

CHAPTER VIII. Nasib Baik yang Dialami Ksatria Berani dalam Petualangan Mengerikan dan Tak Terduga dari Kincir Angin

Don Quixote, having encountered the windmills, which he took for giants, charged at them with his lance. But as he approached, a sudden gust of wind caught the sail of one of the mills, and striking his lance, broke it into pieces, and throwing him and his horse to the ground.

Sancho Panza ran to help his master, who was covered with dust and confusion. "Did I not tell you, sir," said he, "that those were windmills, and not giants? You must have been bewitched to mistake them for such."

"Hold thy peace, Sancho," replied Don Quixote, "for thou knowest nothing of such matters. That enchanter Freston, who has robbed me of my lance, has changed these giants into windmills to deprive me of the honor of vanquishing them."
